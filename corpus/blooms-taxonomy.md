# Bloom's Revised Taxonomy

> A six-level model of cognitive complexity, from recalling a fact to creating
> something new, used to tell shallow learning from deep learning in what someone
> actually says about their own work.

## Core Idea

The original 1956 taxonomy ranked educational objectives from simple to complex:
Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, Evaluation. The 2001
revision (Anderson & Krathwohl) turned the nouns into verbs — Remember,
Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create — and swapped the order of the top
two, arguing that creating something new is a higher cognitive act than judging
something that already exists.

The levels aren't just a difficulty ladder; they describe genuinely different
mental operations. Remembering is retrieval. Understanding is restating in your
own words. Applying is using a known procedure in a new situation. Analyzing is
breaking something into parts and seeing how they relate — this is where a causal
explanation lives ("X failed because Y"). Evaluating is making a judgment against
a criterion. Creating is assembling parts into a new whole — a generalized
principle, a new design, a process someone didn't have before.

The practical diagnostic: most people's self-reports of what they learned sit at
Remember or Understand ("I learned about X", "I now know that Y exists") even
when the actual work involved real Analysis or Evaluation. The gap between what
was *done* and what was *said* is exactly what a reflection should close.

## How Growth OS Applies It

`agents/reflection-scorer.md`'s **D1 — Cognitive Depth** dimension (0–3 points)
is this taxonomy directly: a reflection scores 0–1 for bare event description
(Remember/Understand — "I added X, Y happened, I fixed it"), 2 for a real causal
chain (Analyze — "X failed because Y led to Z"), and 3 only when the person both
judges the past decision (Evaluate) *and* derives a generalizable principle
(Create) — not just "fix this bug differently" but "this class of bugs needs a Y
approach". The rubric explicitly caps the score at 1 when the only cause offered
is effort-based ("I should have been more careful") rather than structural,
since that's Bloom's lowest level dressed up as an explanation.
