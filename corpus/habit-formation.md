# Habit Formation & Implementation Intentions

> Intending to do something is a weak predictor of doing it. Specifying exactly
> *when and where* you'll do it — an "implementation intention" — measurably
> closes the gap between intention and action.

## Core Idea

Behavioral science distinguishes a *goal intention* ("I want to exercise more")
from an *implementation intention* ("I will exercise Tuesday and Thursday at
7am before work"). Peter Gollwitzer's research found that simply forming an
if-then plan tied to a specific situational cue — a day, a time, a trigger
event — substantially increases follow-through compared to the goal intention
alone, because the plan pre-commits the decision before willpower or
motivation is needed in the moment. The vague version ("soon," "this week,"
"when I get a chance") leaves the decision of *when* to be made fresh every
time, which is precisely when it gets deferred.

Separately, habit-loop research (popularized by Charles Duhigg, building on
decades of psychology and neuroscience) describes habits as a three-part
loop: a cue, a routine, and a reward, where repetition of the loop makes the
routine increasingly automatic and cue-triggered rather than
decision-dependent. The practical implication for any recurring practice (a
drill, a review ritual, a reflection habit) is the same as Gollwitzer's
finding: a habit that depends on a fresh decision each time is fragile; one
anchored to a specific, recurring cue is not.

## How Growth OS Applies It

`agents/weekly-reviewer.md`'s **W5 — System Health** (0–1) applies the
implementation-intention distinction as a binary "calendar test": a committed
drill with a specific day and approximate time ("PR rewrite: Thursday 7–8pm")
scores 1; a goal intention without a cue ("I'll do it soon," "sometime this
week") scores 0, regardless of sincerity. The weekly interview's W-Q4 question
asks for exactly this — day plus approximate time, not a vague timeframe — and
the `homework-log.md` file exists to hold the recurring drills themselves as
standing commitments that carry forward until done, rather than being
re-decided from scratch every week.
