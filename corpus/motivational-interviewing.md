# Motivational Interviewing

> A conversational style for evoking someone's own reasons for change, rather
> than supplying reasons or advice from outside — because change that someone
> argues themselves into tends to stick; change argued *at* them tends not to.

## Core Idea

Motivational Interviewing (MI) was developed originally in addiction treatment,
where confrontational "tell them the facts and they'll change" approaches
reliably failed, and has since spread into coaching, healthcare, and
education. Its central insight: people are generally ambivalent about change,
holding reasons both for and against it simultaneously, and the direction a
conversation pushes that ambivalence matters enormously. If the *interviewer*
argues for change, the other person — defending their side of the
ambivalence — ends up arguing *against* it, out loud, which entrenches the
status quo. If instead the interviewer asks open questions and reflects back
what the person says, the person articulates their *own* case for change,
which is what actually predicts follow-through.

Practically, MI relies on open-ended questions (not yes/no, not leading),
reflective listening (restating what was heard, including the feeling under
it, to confirm understanding and let the person hear their own reasoning
restated), and resisting the urge to supply the "right" answer, advice, or
diagnosis — even when the interviewer is confident they know it. "Change talk"
— the person's own statements in favor of change — is the signal MI
practitioners listen for and gently reinforce; "sustain talk" — their own
arguments for staying the same — is acknowledged without being argued against.

## How Growth OS Applies It

`agents/onboarding-agent.md` names Motivational Interviewing as one of its
core lenses and encodes its behavior directly: "Ask ONE question at a time,"
"choose the follow-up based on what the user actually said," and when an
answer reveals a contradiction, "name it gently" rather than correct it
("Mencionaste X pero también Y — ¿cómo conviven?") — a reflective, not
confrontational, move. The agent's explicit caveat ("This interview is a tool
for orientation, not assessment... do not suggest the user 'should' want
anything different from what they said") is MI's core discipline: the
system's job is to help someone articulate their own goals and blockers
clearly, never to argue them toward goals the system thinks they should have.
