# Flow Theory

> The state of complete absorption in an activity — losing track of time,
> action and awareness merging — is a reliable signal of a near-perfect match
> between a challenge and someone's skill level, and that match is exactly
> where someone's real engagement lives.

## Core Idea

Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's research, built on interviews with people across
wildly different domains (rock climbers, chess players, surgeons, composers)
about their peak engaged experiences, converged on a consistent description
people gave of their best moments at an activity: complete concentration,
merging of action and awareness, a sense of control without anxiety about
losing it, loss of self-consciousness, and a distorted sense of time (it
either flies by or stretches out). He named this state "flow."

The mechanism that produces flow is a specific balance: the challenge of the
task has to closely match the person's skill level. Too far below skill, and
the result is boredom. Too far above, and the result is anxiety. Flow lives in
a narrow channel between the two, and critically, that channel *moves* as
skill grows — an activity that produced flow at a lower skill level stops
producing it once it becomes too easy, which means sustaining flow in a craft
over time requires continually finding the next calibrated challenge, not
repeating what used to feel engaging.

The practical use of flow as a diagnostic: rather than asking someone
abstractly what they're motivated by, asking for a specific recent moment of
losing track of time doing something produces a much more reliable signal of
genuine intrinsic engagement — self-report about motivation in the abstract is
easy to rationalize; a concrete flow memory is harder to fake and easier to
act on.

## How Growth OS Applies It

`agents/onboarding-agent.md`'s Phase 5 follow-up for a mastery-type answer asks
directly for a flow memory: "¿Cuándo fue la última vez que perdiste la noción
del tiempo haciendo esto? ¿Qué estabas haciendo exactamente?" — the agent notes
this explicitly as mapping "the Ikigai 'lo que amas' axis concretely" through a
Csikszentmihalyi lens. The saved profile's `flow_signal` field holds that
concrete activity or context, intended as a durable reference point: a signal
of where this person's skill-challenge balance currently sits, useful for
noticing later whether new work is being chosen anywhere near that channel.
